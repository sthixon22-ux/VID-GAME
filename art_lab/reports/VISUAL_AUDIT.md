# Verdant Oath — visual asset audit and character-pipeline comparison

Reviewed October 20, 2026 (America/Phoenix). Tested with Godot **4.6.3**, OpenGL Compatibility, Linux/software rendering. Snapshot: local commit `0276ea1`, which includes the prior 0.9 MakeHuman/KayKit hero experiment on top of the 0.8 game. This audit preserves that working snapshot rather than reverting gameplay to an earlier release.

**Recommendation: Pipeline B for the intended anime character standard, using an originally authored fantasy avatar and Quaternius's verified free humanoid animations. Pipeline A is the stronger immediately usable free fantasy-outfit reference, but its downloaded faces are less expressive and its Standard wardrobe is small. Neither stock sample is a finished original protagonist.** Production replacement has not begun.

Open [`art_lab/comparison.tscn`](../comparison.tscn) and press **F6**. F5 remains the game. See [lab controls](../README.md), [source/permission/hash manifest](../SOURCES.json) and [per-file model inventory](asset_inventory.json) / [texture inventory](texture_inventory.json).

## 1. Existing art inventory and weakest points

The JSON inventory lists every inspected GLB/glTF/VRM, source category, source joint counts, triangle count, materials, image count, animation names and morph count. Counts below describe the checked-in files, not guessed pack marketing totals.

| Asset group | What is actually in the project | Authorship and visual assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Original KayKit Adventurers | Vendor Knight/Rogue/Mage: 5,683–6,952 triangles, 41-joint rigs, 76 embedded actions each including rest/reference data | Professionally authored CC0 by Kay Lousberg. Consistent, efficient stylized silhouettes and a useful animation family. Chibi proportions, painted palette faces and lack of facial morphs conflict with the selected detailed anime direction. Do not make these the new body standard. |
| Current custom hero variants | Three 23,200-triangle human bodies, 41-joint KayKit rigs, no embedded painted textures or facial morphs; Knight/Rogue/Mage contain 76/152/228 animation entries | Hybrid derivatives generated by `build_natural_hero.py`: MakeHuman topology, original hair/eye assembly and automated skinning, retaining KayKit animation. Later GLBs duplicate earlier imported action sets; larger files do not mean more distinct usable motions. |
| Equipment | 30 generated GLBs, 175,822 triangles combined; sword/shield/chest models plus runtime primitive attachments | Original placeholder geometry, not a professionally authored armor family. Metal/trim materials help readability, but silhouette, hand grips, armor fit and articulated shoulders vary. Static chest/head attachments cannot replace fully skinned modular armor. Some primitive modular pieces are still created in `hero_visual.gd`. |
| Companions and meadow creature | Eight generated models (six permanent companions, loan wolf and bristleback), 123,510 triangles combined; no skin rigs or embedded animations | Original generated segmented geometry. Pose articulation, limb anatomy, facial appeal, wing deformation and material vocabulary vary. Node rotation/wobble is not fluid authored quadruped animation. This is among the weakest groups despite its polygon count. |
| Goblin, Mossling, Warden | Three generated models, 41,496 triangles combined; no skin rigs or embedded animations | Original placeholders. Different shapes communicate encounter roles, but anatomy, expressions, locomotion and combat transitions fall short of the target. |
| Medieval Village | Quaternius selected walls/roofs/windows/props with painted plaster/brick/wood/tile normal/roughness maps | Professionally authored credited CC0 pack. A stronger cohesive environment starting point. The trim atlas and block assembly need consistent scale and exposure; a character replacement alone will not fix the environment. |
| Terrain, fabric and foliage | Original generated albedo/normal/roughness maps, alpha-card branches, wind/water shaders and mostly flat ground | Functional placeholders. Repetition, noisy distant cutouts, flat terrain, loose prop placement and material scale reduce depth. Professionally authored foliage/terrain materials should be a separate coherent pass. |
| Combat presentation | KayKit hero clips plus project effects, cameras, damage numbers; creatures use segmented motion | Separation from deterministic combat is already useful. Existing per-action VFX do not establish weapon-specific full-body animation quality, foot contact, recovery or creature rig quality. |

The most useful first replacement is **one properly authored humanoid with face, skinning, outfit and grip standards**, followed by one genuinely skinned creature. Adding more unrelated model packs first would amplify inconsistency.

## 4. Pipeline evaluation

| Criterion | A — Quaternius modular fantasy | B — anime VRM-compatible characters |
|---|---|---|
| Intended art direction | Better fantasy materials/equipment fit than the current placeholders; more angular/realistic than the selected anime style | Stronger appealing anime facial proportions and expression potential; sample costumes are not final fantasy costumes |
| Facial expressiveness | No morphs in the tested head; expressions require additional authored face work | Imported facial/viseme/blink controls actually work; dialogue driving and timed expressions still need implementation |
| Customization | Existing skin/eye/hair meshes, two tested outfit sets and authored part visibility; free wardrobe coverage is limited | Editable textures/morphs possible, but delivered `.vrm` is a finished avatar, not an editable `.vroid` source project. Original source files and a body-family standard are needed |
| Modular armor | Matching Quaternius body/outfit rigs give a strong reference; supplier says hide the underlying body except the head to avoid clipping | Arbitrary VRM outfits are not plug-and-play. Need a consistent body, bind pose, skinned slots, body masks and weight transfer by an artist |
| Sword/shield | Native hand bones, real tested attachments | Native humanoid hand mapping, real tested attachments; per-avatar grip calibration remains |
| Animation | Quaternius libraries retarget after explicit profile mapping; motion quality is useful | Same libraries work on the humanoid profile, but secondary bones/constraints and garment deformation require individual review |
| License scalability | Use pinned verified free Standard assets; current QAL/edition differences mean every future pack needs its own check | Per-model permission metadata and attribution must travel with each avatar/part; no assumptions about third-party VRoid/VRM downloads |
| All twelve classes | The rendered outfit does not impose any class restriction. Free stock cannot visually represent all class identities by itself | One neutral anime body can support all classes through equipment/costume presentation; current samples likewise do not provide twelve class wardrobes |
| Production readiness | Closest free fantasy clothing prototype, but not the selected final expressive-face standard | Best long-term visual direction, but requires an original protagonist, fantasy armor, constraint cleanup, animation polish and performance work |

**Recommended combination:** an original anime humanoid family and expressive face under Pipeline B, with a documented Godot humanoid rig standard and verified Quaternius animation clips as the initial motion source. Use Pipeline A to benchmark skinned armor organization and material detail. Do not simply place Quaternius armor onto a differently proportioned VRM body or combine incompatible creature styles and call the art unified.

## 5. First production-quality character pipeline — after review

1. Approve a full-body and face target from the actual comparison. Author or commission one original stylized anime base with source files, clean topology, UVs, eye/mouth/blink morphs, consistent proportions and complete commercial/redistribution rights. Keep the licensed stock samples as references rather than the canonical protagonist.
2. Establish a neutral humanoid rest pose, names/profile and units. Keep the existing deterministic combat independent of rendering. Import the original avatar through the tested VRM/MToon path and document supported secondary-bone behavior.
3. Define a visual profile resource: avatar scene, canonical hand/head sockets, per-weapon grip offsets, animation library, expression bindings, outfit slots and body-hide masks. Select visuals by equipped item IDs and appearance—not by hardcoding Paladin/Ranger/Druid or by restricting class selection.
4. Produce one fitted travel outfit, then one fitted fantasy armor set on that same body. Test eight independent equipment slots, skinning through shoulders/elbows/hips, two-handed/shield appearance rules and the existing live equipment menu. Retain source meshes/weights so future body variants can be conformed properly.
5. Calibrate the sword and shield sockets using authored prop origins. Polish idle, start/stop, walk/run, sword attack/recovery, shield guard/impact, cast/recovery, hit, victory and defeat through an AnimationTree. Existing intent-to-animation adapters should remain presentation-only.
6. Add facial dialogue/blink drivers and per-ability effects. Confirm head, hair, robe and sleeve behavior during all required clips on the actual target renderer. Constraint-heavy avatars must be fixed or simplified before they pass this gate.
7. Set a texture/shader/outline palette and triangle/draw-call/LOD budget across hero, one enemy, one companion and the village. Source suitable original or licensed skinned creatures in that visual family. Resolve the goblin source gap before replacing it.
8. Only then replace one production visual adapter at a time. Preserve all twelve classes, three-class creation, proficiency/hand rules, skill/companion systems, quests, mastery and save schema; rerun the existing gameplay suite after each swap.

## 6. Technical risks and practical limits

- **Source completeness / license editions:** Standard is not Source/Pro; two available outfit sets do not constitute a complete armor library. A mirror's generic CC0 label is insufficient when the actual pack license says otherwise.
- **Rest axes / skin binds:** UAL1 has DEF-style bones; UAL2 and the clothes use another naming scheme. The lab supplies separate import bone maps. Shared names alone do not make rest axes, bind matrices or proportions compatible.
- **VRM constraints:** The Twist sample visibly exhibits sleeve/twist artifacts despite valid humanoid tracks. Seed-san is a cleaner facial/body reference but has modern clothing and an optional robot accessory. The importer itself documents constraint/retarget limitations.
- **Retargeted motion is not final choreography:** Feet, hips, hand closure, grip offsets, robe/armor clipping and transition blending need artist review. The lab loops clips for comparison; it does not pretend to deliver a finished combat AnimationTree.
- **MToon fidelity:** Actual toon materials render in Compatibility. Neutral exposure was calibrated across all three viewports to avoid skin clipping. Exact reference-renderer parity, production lighting and Forward+/mobile GPUs have not been certified.
- **Modular armor:** Whole-avatar VRMs may merge body/clothes, and mismatched body families cause clipping. Preserve editable source and planned body masks; do not solve every armor slot by attaching a rigid block to a bone.
- **Performance:** Source humanoids are roughly 22k–45k triangles, with multiple materials, face morphs and secondary bones. Three comparison viewports are intentionally an inspection workload, not a gameplay performance benchmark. Production needs measured LOD, texture memory, animation and spring-bone budgets. Exclude the review-only `art_lab/` assets/tests/screenshots from eventual production export presets; they are not a shipping content dependency.
- **Environment cohesion:** A better face does not fix flat terrain, noisy foliage, oversized props or inconsistent PBR/toon lighting. Those need a separate, consistent environment pass.
- **Goblin gap:** No suitably licensed, species-matched professional replacement was cleared for this source bundle. The scene marks the replacement absent rather than fabricating an integration or substituting an unrelated model.

## 7. Validation

The lab validates moving skeletal tracks, hand-attachment motion, expressions modifying actual morph weights, independent outfit pieces, all twelve definitions, and unchanged hero state. It verifies SHA-256 hashes of every production script, scene, gameplay-data file, model and texture captured before this work. Production models have not been replaced.

The existing nine suites pass **985 checks** with the new import plugins installed, including the real creation-to-ending/postgame flow, all twelve class starts, all 1,320 ordered class combinations, equipment/hand rules, combat/Auto parity, companions and save behavior. Lab-specific checks and clean import/render results are recorded in [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md).
