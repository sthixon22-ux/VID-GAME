# Verdant Oath — Full Campaign Bible (Draft 0.1)

Status: proposed creative canon, ready for review. This **extends** the existing *Bell Beneath the Woods* demo; it does not replace it.

## Unchanging promises
- Original, single-player, stylized 3D fantasy RPG; preserve the existing Godot game and established opening.
- The hero can choose any one primary and two distinct secondary classes from the existing twelve. The bloodline reveal must work for every class and appearance.
- The heroine/hero is the lost child of the Demon King and was hidden by their mother. Reveal the father around dungeon 8, not in the opening.
- Introduce the human king early, give both rulers strengths and flaws, and allow a **deliberate, late** allegiance decision.
- Twelve **main-story** dungeons, including the existing sanctuary as #1; #12 has alternate destinations, encounters and final bosses depending on allegiance.
- Climate-appropriate enemies; town shops with regional specializations; preserve the existing ten-normal-victory monster mastery system.
- Preserve save data, quest flags, companion choices and the existing gameplay framework.

## Logline
The Bell of Willowbrook rings after a century of silence. An unknown wanderer restores its guardian and becomes entangled in the fate of two rival crowns. Only after traveling across a scarred continent do they learn that the demon sovereign is their father, and that their mother stole away a royal heir to prevent a war. The final choice is not whether the bloodline exists, but what responsibility the hero accepts.

## Historical truth (authorial draft)
Generations ago humans and demonkind shared passage through ancient Rootgates, protected by guardians including the Hollow Warden. The human crown shut the gates after a devastating border war; demon settlements were stranded, and some human borderlands were destroyed in retaliation. Both courts have since taught selective histories. The Demon King, **Vaelor**, wishes to reopen all gates by force, end his people's isolation and claim dominion over human territories. The human king, **Aldren**, seeks to keep civilians safe, but conceals evidence that his dynasty profited from the original closure. Neither side is inherently righteous or entirely false.

The hero's mother, **Seren**, was an emissary and wardkeeper who loved Vaelor yet rejected his determination to use their child as a living key to control the Rootgates. She fled, hid the child's royal nature behind old warding rites, and left fragmentary records across the world so the child could someday choose freely. Her fate is an ongoing mystery: initially missing, later discovered to have survived in secrecy at great personal cost. Avoid a simplistic rescue or death until her Act IV arc is fully written.

The inherited power, **Emberblood**, manifests as resonance with bells, warding stones and gates, independent of the player's primary/secondary classes. It is not inherently evil, and every build can unlock the same story interactions. Player choices influence how it is *used*, not which class is viable.

## Five-act shape
1. **The Bell (Dungeon 1–2):** Preserve Mira, Moonleaf, Whisperwood, Sunmeadow, the Hollow Warden, and the companion bond. Introduce Aldren through a royal audience, not a retroactive rewrite. Investigate a second ward failure.
2. **Fractured Borders (3–5):** Desert archives, mountain arms trade, jungle living wards. The player sees conflicting accounts of a past treaty and encounters Vaelor's agents with diverse motives.
3. **The Sealed Name (6–8):** Frozen records, a sea-gate warning, hidden subterranean lineage vault. Dungeon 8 reveals Vaelor's parentage and Seren's role.
4. **Two Thrones (9–11):** Learn the costs of reopening and maintaining the Rootgates, confront both courts' complicity, meet Seren, and acquire a means to choose another path rather than obey destiny.
5. **Inheritance (12):** At a clear, warned commitment point choose to oppose Vaelor or oppose Aldren. The last stronghold and final battle must differ. Endings show consequences for civilians and both realms. Maintain branch-aware postgame.

## Major characters
- **Mira** — Willowbrook mentor and keeper of the opening oath; the phrase “what we protect” anchors the game's theme.
- **The Hollow Warden** — restored forest guardian, witness to a missing part of the old treaty.
- **King Aldren** — principled defender of the human realm who knowingly maintains a flawed peace.
- **Demon King Vaelor** — charismatic father, an advocate for his isolated people whose coercive strategy risks catastrophe.
- **Seren** — mother, wardkeeper and agent of her child's freedom.
- **Captain Rowan, Artisan Nessa, Keeper Oren** — retain existing quests and village roles.
- **The Herald of Ash** (working title) — midgame antagonist who exploits human and demonic grievances to destabilize the gates, providing adversaries without making every demon an enemy.

## Settlements and their economies (proposals)
- **Willowbrook** (existing): basic first gear and recovery, preserve the current outfitter.
- **Reedcross** (Mirefen): antidotes, spears, waterproof light armor; Ranger/Druid niches.
- **Qasira Caravanserai** (Sahir): bows, daggers, light armor, travel tools; Ranger/Rogue/Assassin niches.
- **Kharholt Forge** (mountains): swords, axes, hammers, heavy armor, shields; Warrior/Barbarian/Paladin niches.
- **Leafhaven** (jungle): staves, sickles, nature charms, companion goods; Druid/Healer niches.
- **Snowmere** (tundra): frost-resistant gear, practical medium armor, elemental warding.
- **Galecrest Port** (coast): polearms, crossbows, lightning wards, Monk/Ranger equipment.
- **Vesper Archive** (ancient border city): tomes, magical staves, ritual relics; Wizard/Sorcerer/Healer niches.
- **Cinderfall Encampment** (volcanic): fire warding, scythes, volcanic blades; Necromancer/Sorcerer niches.
- **Mournglen Refuge** (highlands): charms, defensive wards and anti-curse equipment.
- **Black March Outpost** (demonic frontier): advanced multi-class tools, but grounded in the area's story.
- **Capital Aurel** (human royal city): prestigious and politically controlled armories.

All **major towns** need distinct weapon and armor shops, inns/recovery and local quests. Class emphases should reflect the **twelve implemented classes**; Sage is not currently playable.

## Encounter ecology
Forest goblins / mosslings / bristlebacks → marsh leeches and reed wraiths → desert scarabs and sand serpents → mountain sentries and iron beasts → jungle thorncats and venomous idols → tundra frostlings → coastal storm harpies → vault memory sentinels → volcanic ash fiends → highland oath-echoes → border demon legions. A demon is not automatically evil; include neutral and allied demonkind. Design boss mechanics per biome, not HP inflation.

Use **regional level bands** with bounded adaptation, not universal rubber-band scaling. Earlier regions must become easier on return trips. Keep single-enemy combat viable until multi-enemy battles are deliberately designed and tested.

## Bloodline ability design (narrative system, not a thirteenth class)
1. *Resonance* — detect/activate ancient seals.
2. *Emberwake* — see fragments of lost memories.
3. *Royal Claim* — traverse old Rootgates.
4. *Chosen Oath* — late-game use of power reflecting the final allegiance.
No combat advantage should trivialize one of the twelve classes. These are provisional effects requiring implementation review.

## Branch design
Choice must be **explicit and reversible until confirmation**, made near #12. Branch flags persist. Anti-Vaelor path leads to the demonic citadel, father confrontation and political aftermath. Pro-Vaelor path leads to capital Aurel, human king confrontation and aftermath. Both should have full playable dungeon, boss, epilogue and postgame flags. Neither ending is the “wrong” one.

## Scope discipline
Build region-by-region after a playable transition from the current demo. Do not auto-populate 12 unfinished scenes and call the campaign done. Retain existing NPC names, dialogue and mechanics; avoid spoiling the ancestry in Act I UI or quest text.

## Implemented Act I continuation (Chapter 2 alpha)

Aldren is personally overseeing Reedcross’s relief effort after the forest guardian’s restoration. Messenger Elian introduces the hero; the king supplies a travel purse and asks the hero to listen to Warden Iona. Three causeway readings expose a trapped inward current, leading to the Sunken Reliquary. Draining the western basin and diverting the east flow frees the Keeper of the Drowned Oath. Iona identifies a damaged covenant tablet pointing toward the Emberglass archives. Aldren promises causeway repairs and rewards the hero. No parentage/bloodline reveal occurs here. Sella’s medicine gathering and Jory’s causeway patrol give the flood a local civilian cost. This establishes Act I’s playable continuation; the later desert region remains unimplemented.
